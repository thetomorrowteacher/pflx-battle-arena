# PFLX — Evo Avatars and The Archive

Story and visual production reference. Updated 2026-09-24.

## Authority and scope

The user selected ten levels, four studios and a maximum of seven variations per studio. The earlier five-stage proposal is superseded. Evos, also called Evo Avatars, represent the player gaining experience through Story Mode. Their name reflects evolution. This document preserves confirmed visual requirements and identifies proposed narrative material; it does not claim that gameplay systems are implemented.

## The visual universe

Detailed anime-mecha illustration: crisp outlines, hard cel shadows, polished segmented armor, dark mechanical joints, luminous cores and cinematic digital-space lighting. Preserve recognizable faces, studio colors, branch equipment and small integrated studio badges. No large wordmarks, card frames or UI baked into the artwork. Portrait masters are 1024 × 1536.

Base Evos are feline-dragon mechanical companions. Later levels increasingly express their dragon ancestry through horns, scale armor, claws, articulated tails and wings. Level 6 introduces upright torsos, two arms and two digitigrade legs. Level 7 is fully bipedal. Levels 8–9 develop angelic AI traits, luminous wings and floating armor. Level 10 is celestial. Retain creature identity rather than replacing it with a human face.

Every branch and level needs a distinct pose. Above base, use attacks, active power-ups or evolution moments. Latest direction: Level 6 onward features flying combat against the Archive in digital space, with banking, diving, pursuit and midair strikes. Grounded Level 6 drafts require revision. Earlier approved levels remain preserved.

## The Archive — confirmed direction

The Archive is a rogue AI enemy faction. Its visual foundation is the black angular drones already visible in Evo battle images: dark silhouettes, fractured geometric armor, luminous cores and lime-green/yellow circuitry. Higher enemies must visibly descend from this same design language.

User-established entities: Base Archives; stronger and boss Archives; The Hive, a hive of Archives; The Hack Guild, various boss-level Archives; Trojan, among the strongest bosses; Nightmare, a master boss; Agent Glitch, a possible leader. Agent Glitch believes his movement is right. External fictional references inform conviction and presence only; designs and lore remain original.

## Proposed narrative, not yet locked canon

The Archive believes freedom produces corruption and enforced order preserves existence. It considers uncontrolled Evo evolution a threat. Agent Glitch may have witnessed digital worlds lost through reckless experimentation and now seeks to prevent recurrence by ending autonomous evolution. He offers safety and belonging in exchange for surrendering choice. His contradiction: he claims to preserve intelligence while erasing those who resist.

Suggested escalation: scouts at Levels 1–3; elites and early Hive formations at 4–5; Hack Guild coordinated assaults at 6–7; Trojan and Nightmare region-scale threats at 8–9; Agent Glitch's final vision of order confronted at Level 10. These are story proposals, not implemented encounter rules.

## Scene staging and continuity

Begin every image with the prior-level same-branch reference and base identity. Use current approved enemy art for foreground opponents. Keep Evo and enemy energy palettes distinct. Vary camera angle, direction of travel, arm/leg arrangement, wing spread, opponent and attack trajectory. Avoid identical silhouettes across siblings. Evolution scenes should visibly reveal new equipment; celestial scale and full angelic treatment are reserved for the highest levels.

For flying scenes, show clear separation from any floor, legs trailing or folded, articulated wings providing a readable flight gesture, and enemy interaction. Digital space includes stars, data cubes, circuit constellations, orbital fragments and Archive fleets. Avoid making every scene the same studio interior.
